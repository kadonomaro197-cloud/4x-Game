→ How the ground becomes the fight — what's real

You don't pick a battle's environment from a menu — the location picks it for you, and the AI reads the same thing off the same hex. But be exact about what "environment" means to the engine today. The ground resolver reads two things; the space resolver reads nothing about location at all.

So "wired to the auto-resolver" means the GROUND resolver, and it means two live wires: the terrain triangle and the surface-attrition bleed. Everything else this view shows is either data the game holds but combat ignores, or a design proposal. That honesty is the point of the grades — the panel to the right never blurs the three.

⚑ Two real findings this surfaced

1 · The only surface-hazard armour the game can build is the sealed suit — and it only stops vacuum and toxic air. The sealed-systems component folds into a unit's EnvironmentalResistance for Vacuum and ToxicAtmosphere only. There is no component that resists fire, cryo, or corrosive attrition — so on Venus, a fully sealed marine still burns from the fire tornadoes (≈23/hr) and the corrosive superstorm (25/hr); sealing only saves them the 3/hr toxic bleed. The engine supports heat/corrosion resistance (the map is keyed by every hazard kind) — the component that writes it was never built. A "you can't hold this world without the right kit" decision is one component away.
2 · Mercury renders as a temperate barren world, not a scorched one. The terrain generator reads AtmosphereDB.SurfaceTemperature — but Mercury has no atmosphere blob, so the generator falls back to a default 15 °C and never sees its real heat. So Mercury's surface comes out barren/desert/highlands (temperate-dry), while its hazard layer correctly reads airless → vacuum. A body's terrain climate and its hazard climate can disagree when it has no atmosphere record. (Luna dodges this because it has a real baked map.)
