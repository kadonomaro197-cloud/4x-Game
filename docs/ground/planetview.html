A planet, exactly as the game models it

Every world here is a real Sol body read from the game's data files. Earth, Mars and Luna draw their actual baked surface maps — the same tables the engine samples — so you see real continents, oceans, the Tharsis volcanoes, the lunar maria, and the polar ice caps. Every terrain number is what the ground combat resolver actually applies; every world hazard is what the physics-driven generator actually produces; every resource node is a located mineral deposit the way the engine seeds them — terrain-weighted, never in the sea or the ice, and two-tier: the regional hex flags that ore is present, and clicking in pinpoints the exact mini-hexes that hold it. A whole developed civilization spans Earth, its capital grown into a super-city (an ecumenopolis of stacked arcologies, an entertainment strip, a financial exchange and a metro police force) with four supporting provinces placed on the ground that suits each — mines in the hills, foundry on the plains, farms on the delta, the shield on a ridge. Click a province and each mini-hex is a district — a cluster of real installations whose outputs sum — and scroll below the map for the civilization's books: ~27 M people, and two live gauges built from the real morale coefficients — morale → migration and legitimacy → rebellion — plus commerce income. You can drop a float tag (a name of your choosing) on any hex. Pick a world, click a hex, read the fight and the ground beneath it — each effect graded by whether the code reads it today, only holds the data, or is a design proposal.

→ How the ground becomes the fight — what's real

You don't pick a battle's environment from a menu — the location picks it for you, and the AI reads the same thing off the same hex. But be exact about what "environment" means to the engine today. The ground resolver reads two things; the space resolver reads nothing about location at all.

⚑ Two real findings this surfaced

1 · The sealed suit only stopped vacuum + toxic air — so a fully sealed marine still burned on Venus. ✔ NOW FIXED (2026-08-09): the Environmental Hardening component is built. The gap: sealed-systems folds into a unit's EnvironmentalResistance for Vacuum + ToxicAtmosphere only, so on Venus a fully sealed marine still burned from the fire tornadoes and the corrosive superstorm; sealing saved only the toxic bleed. The engine already supported heat/corrosion resistance (the map is keyed by every hazard kind) — the component that writes it was just never built. It is now: GroundHardeningAtb / the base-mod environmental-hardening component — one Hardening dial the assembler folds into EnvironmentalResistance {HeatDamage, CorrosiveDamage}. HeatDamage is the engine's one thermal effect for both fire AND cryo, so one component hardens against fire, cryo, and corrosive — the thermal/corrosive twin of the seal, mounted independently (Venus wants both; an ice moon wants this). Full six-point registration + a gauge (GroundHardeningComponentTests: a hardened unit outlasts both an unhardened AND a merely-sealed twin on a fire+corrosive world). The "you can't hold this world without the right kit" decision is now in the game. (One rung still on the generator side: a cold-world cryo hazard isn't emitted yet — the component already resists it the moment one is.)
2 · Mercury renders as a temperate barren world, not a scorched one. The terrain generator reads AtmosphereDB.SurfaceTemperature — but Mercury has no atmosphere blob, so the generator falls back to a default 15 °C and never sees its real heat. So Mercury's surface comes out barren/desert/highlands (temperate-dry), while its hazard layer correctly reads airless → vacuum. A body's terrain climate and its hazard climate can disagree when it has no atmosphere record. (Luna dodges this because it has a real baked map.)

Ground truth · design study, 2026-08-10 (rev-P — THE CAPITAL GROWN INTO A SUPER-CITY + THE METROPOLIS-vs-VILLAGE DIALS, wired at the source. New Terra is now an ecumenopolis (~21 M) with the new civic districts — 🎭 Entertainment, 🏦 Financial, 🚔 Metro Police — inside a ~27 M civilization, and the dashboard reads TWO GAUGES built from the real ColonyMoraleDB coefficients: MORALE (~94/100 → migration +4.4%/mo, a magnet) and LEGITIMACY (75/100 → firmly governed), plus COMMERCE (660 k cr/mo). The city runs at ~86% employment — near full — via the service-employment coefficient as a DENSITY dial (k=0.5; a metropolis employs nearly its whole workforce in services, which is the only lever that moves employment since arcologies add people AND jobs together). WEATHER now affects morale: a NEW transient morale line fed live by the rolling-storm system — roll the weather and morale dips as a storm crosses the city (−6/province), recovering as it passes. The five civic dials were derived from a grounded 15-agent source survey and wired at the SOURCE designer (civicderived.html): Jobs (BUILD-NOW — the dead employment term's first producer), Entertainment→a new uncapped CivicAmenity morale line, Weather→a new transient morale line, Law-enforcement→a new PublicOrder legitimacy input (holds off RebellionDB), Trade→a new ColonyCommerce category on the live Ledger. On rev-O's five-province civilization + source crew recalibration, rev-N's float tags, rev-L's two-tier resources, rev-K's terrain-affinity, rev-I's grid + potato budget, real surface maps, per-hex hazards, weather + day/night). No engine code, no CI.
