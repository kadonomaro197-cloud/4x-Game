# 01-IO-CHASSIS — Chassis — the assembler's first call, derived
> Source: `docs/Actual HTMLs Of designers/chassisderived.html` · Component-designer door · Census (Phase 1 draft, unverified)

## What this door builds
This door builds the **chassis** — the frame every other component gets bolted onto, and the *first thing the assembler asks for* before it will accept anything else. It re-derives the four chassis that ship today (ship-hull, station-chassis, surface unit frame, building-foundation) as the cells of a grid, so instead of picking a template you answer three plain questions: **where does it have to function (Environment)**, **is it an actor or a place (Unit or Infrastructure)**, and **what is it made of (Substrate)** — then set a **frame size** and your faction's **structural efficiency** with two sliders. A fourth chip picks the **operating band** within the chosen environment.

So the door's shape is **three forced choices plus a fourth band choice, and two continuous sliders** (frame size · structural efficiency), with a third slider (operating envelope) appearing only when the environment has more than one band. Lead idea: the chassis is the thing that *declares* what the entity must survive and supply (heat resistance, a reactor, a magazine, crew or a seated leader, food), so those requirements fall out of the three choices rather than being hard-coded per-assembler. Second real decision beside size: **how many bands the envelope spans** — narrow and cheap, or broad and expensive, with research (`MaxRangeFormula`) capping how wide you may go at all.

A load-bearing detail for anyone reading the numbers: there is a **checkbox (`fix`, checked by default)** that flips the whole panel between the **derived door** (the proposed system) and **what ships today**. Almost every "emergent" readout is only emergent in the derived view; today the same number is a free constant or a free dial.

## D. NOTES / DISCREPANCIES
- **The whole "derived" half is PROPOSED, not shipped.** The `fix` checkbox is the honesty control: with it **unchecked** the panel shows today's reality — size is a **constant on the template**, structural efficiency **does not exist**, the budget is a **free dial**, and there is **no band dial at all** except the one on the `infrastructure` template. Almost every green/emergent readout above is only true in the derived (checked) view. Build order is spelled out as slices **C1–C6** in the footer.
- **Budget is free on all four shipped chassis, and the ceiling is switched ON.** The `<script>` header and the footer state: hull `Mass Budget` (1e3→1e8) and the surface `BaseStrength` (1→1000) / station `Structural Budget` / building `Footprint Budget` are **in no cost formula** ("🔴 NO"). Yet `ShipDesign.cs:285-290` sets `IsValid=false` and `PulsarMainWindow.cs:144` arms the flag, and there are **49 hull references** — so ships mount hulls today against a *free* ceiling. Worse, budget **adds across hulls** (`hullBudget += hull.MassBudget * count`, no "exactly one hull" rule), so stacking two light hulls (50,000 kg) beats one medium (90,000 kg).
- **The latent law:** `budget = 1128 × √(frame size)` (`K_SHIP=1128`), fit to the shipped light+heavy hulls to within **0.9%**; **medium is a 25% outlier** (90,000 vs predicted 112,800) and is the one remaining calibration judgement the developer must accept.
- **CORRECTED axis (flagged in-file):** the "low orbit → high orbit → deep space" altitude axis was **removed** — "nothing about a hull changes between low orbit and high orbit," and altitude is already priced as *fuel* on the launch (`OrbitMath.LowOrbitRadius` = planet radius × 1.1, `FuelCostToOrbit` charges Tsiolkovsky fuel) — the **Logistical** door's bill, not the chassis's. The axis that *does* belong to the chassis is **distance from the star** (three live consumers: corona heat, solar attenuation, `BaseTemperature`). Orbital is **asymmetric**: only the hot end taxes the frame (`BANDCOST.orbital=[1.45,1.0,1.0,1.0]`).
- **Envelope dial already exists in the blueprint system, used on ONE template, where it's free.** `ComponentTemplatePropertyBlueprint` supports `GuiHint.GuiSelectionMinMaxRange` with `PairedPropertyName` + `MaxRangeFormula` (a tech-driven cap on band width). Only the `infrastructure` template uses it — a gravity band (8.8→10.8) and pressure band (0.9→1.1), tech-widened by `tech-infra-{gravity,pressure}-{range,min-extension,max-extension}` (six real techs) — but `infrastructure`'s `Mass` is the constant `1000`, so **widening the envelope costs nothing** today.
- **Dead / free dials on surface frames (flagged):** `Size` on a surface frame is "**dead AND free**" (documented "feeds transport carry-size", zero readers), `BaseHP` is a free **10× toughness** multiplier, `TileFootprint` (1–40) is a "cost with no benefit" so the minimum dominates, and **`research` is `"0"` on every chassis in the game**. All four surface frames author `CrewReq: "0"`, so "mechanical things need people" has **no input** on the cell where mechanical-vs-organic matters most (a battle droid and a conscript cost the same zero people).
- **Anchors for the three empty environments are INVENTED and flagged as such** in the JS (`ANCHOR.water/air/under`, `PRESETS.water/air/under` marked "⚠ invented, no shipped counterpart"). Only `orbital` and `surface` anchors are real (`anchorIsReal()`).
- **Boundary note vs the assembler:** the file's own thesis is that the *requirement gates are already domain-neutral* (`WeaponSupply.*`, `MagazineCapacity_kg`, `DrawsAmmo` in `GroundCombat/`) — so this is not a new engine, it's making the **chassis declare** what each assembler currently hard-codes (`GroundUnitAssembly` = 4 gates; the other three = 1 each). The proposed fix is a 4th member of `IChassisAtb`. That is the seam where this door hands off to the Entity Assembler.
