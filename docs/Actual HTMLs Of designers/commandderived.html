Pulsar4X - component designer - door 5 of the remaining eight - updated 2026-07-31 - the leader grid

Two attributes, and they are the same component built twice - thirteen months apart.

A bridge, an administrative complex, a command berth. Read what each one actually gives the simulation and it is always a seat: something that says what this post commands, how well, and whether the person in it survives the job. The old attribute (AdminSpaceAtb) has the first of those three. The new one (CommandBerthAtb) has all three plus a hazard roll that can kill the occupant.

And the engine says so itself. CommandBerthDB.cs:16 describes the berth's Span dial as "the force size the berth can command - the old ConsoleSpace idea with teeth." That is a comment in the source admitting the newer component is a rebuild of the older one. So the door does not need designing. It needs the two halves merged, with the newer shape winning.

Four dials cost nothing, and one of them switches off this door's own grave rung. A berth's Survivability buys the seated leader's death-risk down to zero, and it appears in no cost formula at all - so the one component in the designer that can kill a named character comes with free immunity.

AdminLevel is an 11-value ladder from Ship to Empire. SiteRole says which kind of field site a berth can work. Only the Role actually gates anything - see below.

How well does it work?

GradeSupportOffice Space

SiteWorkProcessor: Grade x (1 + leaderSkill + Support/100). Office Space sizes a colony's buildable hex map: radius = (office / 100).

Does the occupant survive it?

Survivability

SiteHazard rolls an incident on the seated leader per work step; Survivability buys it down, and 100 zeroes it. This is the only component in the designer that can destroy a named person.

And what the seat holds

One seat per component

AdminSpaceProcessor.ReconcileSeats makes exactly one seat per installed admin component, matched by component name. Not by size, not by level. That single fact is where this door's biggest leak comes from.

So the collapse is: one seat - what it commands - how well - support - survivability (the proposal below cuts span, a fifth dial that turned out to be the second half of how well). The berth already has that exact shape. The bridge and the admin complex are the same component with three of the five missing, and one of the two they do have is unread. Merging them removes an attribute, a template pattern and a whole class of confusion, and adds nothing.

First, the part that is right - and it is the law for the third time

An administrative complex sizes a colony's buildable hex map. Two formulas, one in the template and one in the engine:

Mass = Office Space x 100 - hexMapDB.UpdateMaxRadius: radius = ceil((officeSpace / 100))
=> radius mass, so hex AREA mass. You pay linearly in tonnes for linearly more buildable ground.

Hexes per tonne FALLS as you scale - so there is no dominant setting. That is the 34.7a ladder test passed on a scale dial: you decide how much colony you want, and a bigger one is not free. It is the same square-root relation as the ship hull's mass budget and the same family as T - v = 2P. Nobody wrote it down as a law; it is what these two formulas happen to be when you put them side by side.

One real hazard sitting under it, and it is a known landmine. The door's only output is ColonyHexMapDB, and that blob has zero Clone references - it is the live example named in the root CLAUDE.md landmine index (L12). BaseDataBlob.Clone is virtual with a garbage default (return new object, with a // FIXME: this seems bad in its own source), so a blob that forgets to override it silently becomes a bare System.Object whenever its entity is cloned. So the one thing this door produces does not survive being moved between managers. It has not bitten yet because colonies do not change system - but the Site Engine's located-site work is heading straight at it.

Console Space costs mass x100 and, on a ship, is read by nothing

The same AdminSpaceAtb serves two templates. Its ConsoleSpace field has exactly one reader in the engine:

ColonyHexMapProcessor.ProcessEntity: if (!entity.HasDataBlob<ColonyInfoDB> ||!entity.HasDataBlob<AdminSpaceDB>) return;
The only consumer of ConsoleSpace is gated on ColonyInfoDB - which a ship does not have.

And the seat count does not depend on it either. AdminSpaceProcessor.ReconcileSeats creates exactly one seat per installed component, keyed by the component's name: current.Add((atb.AdminLevel, adminSpace.Name)). Console space is never consulted. So a 1-console bridge and a 20-console bridge both provide one seat - and the 1-console version weighs 100 kg instead of 2,000 kg, needs a quarter the crew, and costs a twentieth the research. The minimum is strictly dominant, and the dial's whole visible promise - a bigger bridge commands more - is not implemented.

Same shape as the fire-control pair from the Sensors door, mirrored. There, two dials cost mass and wrote nothing, so the minimum was a free 16x saving. Here one dial costs mass and writes nothing on one of its two templates, so the minimum is a free 20x saving. The rule that catches both: name the simulation variable the dial writes. If you cannot, and it costs something, the cheapest setting wins and the dial is a tax on players who do not know.

A berth can buy total immunity for its leader, for free

The Command Berth is the best-built new component in the designer - and two of its five dials appear in no cost formula. One of them is the one that matters most.

Survivability is the serious one, because it deletes the door's own grave rung. SiteHazard is a genuinely good mechanic: a dangerous posting rolls, on the seeded system RNG, against the leader seated in the berth - and on a hit the commander is destroyed, the seat is vacated and the standard crew-loss event fires. It is one of very few places in the game where a named character can die from a decision you made. And Survivability, which "meets or exceeds 100 zeroes the risk", is absent from Mass, Volume, CrewReq, ResearchCost, CreditCost, BuildPointCost and ResourceCost. Every berth anyone ever designs will be set to 100.
And look at what Grade does right, because it is the answer for the other three. Grade multiplies work rate x10 across its span, and costs mass x10, crew x10 and research x10 across the same span - so benefit / cost is flat. There is no dominant setting and no free win: Grade is purely how much berth do you want. Put Support and Survivability into the same formula Grade is already in and the whole component is honest - armour and life support for a command post are exactly the kind of thing that should weigh something.

Admin Level names the seat, and nothing else

An 11-value ladder - Ship - TaskUnit - TaskGroup - TaskForce - Fleet - Colony - Planet - SOI - System - Sector - Empire - carried on every admin component, stored on every seat, and consulted by no rule anywhere.

So any commander can be seated in any post. A Ship-level bridge seat will take the officer you meant to run the Empire from, and an Empire-level complex will take an ensign. The dial is free (it is not in any cost formula - only Console Space is), which at least means it is not a tax; but it is a promise the UI displays and the rules do not keep. The template even shapes the ladder sensibly - a bridge is capped at Colony (min 0 max 5), a complex starts there and its ceiling rises with tech-administration-level. Everything about it is right except that nothing reads it.

And this is exactly the hole the berth's Role already fills properly. A berth's Role gates which sites it can work - GetWorkingBerth refuses a mismatched role - so the same idea, one door over, is enforced. Level should gate the same way Role does: a seat accepts a commander whose rank reaches it, and a fleet needs a seat at Fleet level or above to have a commander at all. That is the connection the Governance design (docs/society/GOVERNANCE-AND-DELEGATION-DESIGN.md) is waiting on, and it is a comparison, not a system.

THE PROPOSAL - 26 leaders, as two questions

Everything above is what the door is. This is what it should be, and it is not invented - it is the governance design's own organising rule, which the code already half-implements without knowing it.

docs/society/GOVERNANCE-AND-DELEGATION-DESIGN.md locks the two-axis rule: "Every seat has exactly TWO lines: an ADMIN line - vertical, who you belong to - and a DOMAIN-DIRECTION line - horizontal, what your specialty does." It then lays every leader role on a scope x domain grid and proves no two share a cell.

That grid is a designer door. Scope is one question, Domain is the other, and every leader role in the game is a cell - which means a design you make, not a class somebody hardcodes.

The two axes are already in the code - in two components that cannot see each other

They are the two halves of one grid, built separately. One knows how big a seat is and not what it does; the other knows what it does and not how big. And Span - whose own header calls it "the force size the berth can command... the old ConsoleSpace idea with teeth" - is the missing scope axis, sitting there unread.

And the resolution is already locked, in a different doc. docs/explore/SITE-ENGINE-DESIGN.md 5: "Every site is worked through one component: the Command Berth... the universal socket that plugs any leader into any site - and into fleets/battalions/colonies too - it IS the delegate seat." Its build list (line 157) gives the instruction outright: "fold the three parallel 'assign' slots toward the one seat."

Then the class carries CommanderID and BerthOps sets it. It became the parallel mechanism it was written to avoid. A grep for AdminSpaceDB across Sites/ returns nothing - there is no bridge.

And #3 is worse than a duplicate. A ship's captain is a bare int on ShipInfoDB, written by hand in DefaultStartFactory and friends. No component, no seat, no order - so under One Verb, Both Seats, the AI cannot appoint a ship's captain at all.

Build one - the proposed seat

the proposalThis is what we intend the door to become. Not three templates - one, with the grid as its two doors. Pick a scope and a domain and it names the leader role you just built, the decision that role owns, and what it costs. It reproduces all 26 roles. None of this exists in the engine yet - it is drawn on the shipped berth's own arithmetic, so the numbers are honest.

Two questions and four sliders. Every role in the game becomes a design you make, rather than a class somebody hardcodes.

SCOPE - what altitude does this seat command?

The admin line: who you belong to. It sets the seat's reach, and it multiplies what the seat costs - an empire HQ is not a field post. This collapses AdminLevel's eleven rungs to four, which the governance doc already ruled: "Task Force / Task Group / Task Unit were cut because they didn't own a decision the System Admiral or Fleet Commander didn't already own. Rungs for rungs' sake."

DOMAIN - what specialty does it direct?

The direction line: what your specialty does. It decides which pillar the seat steers and which leader type may sit in it. This widens SiteRole's five to the roster's eight.

The sliders - how much seat

Grade - the rank tier it supports
1 - a junior post10 - flag grade

The dial the shipped berth already prices honestly: x10 capability for x10 mass, crew and research, so benefit / cost stays flat and neither end dominates. Kept exactly as-is - it is the model for the other three.

Support - staff, tools, war room
0 - the officer alone50 - a full staff

Spend mass to make the seated officer better - a flat competence boost folded into the same BonusesDB path research already uses. Now costs mass; today it is in no cost formula at all.

Survivability - how hard the officer is to kill
0 - exposed95 - hardened

Two changes. It now costs mass, and it is capped at 95 - because at 100 the shipped formula zeroes the leader-death roll entirely, and a free immortality switch deletes the door's own grave rung. You can buy your officer very safe; you can never buy them safe.

A scope x domain pair the roster leaves blank on purpose - and the panel says why.

Try this. Walk the four scopes with Space military selected: Fleet Commander -> (blank - ships don't hold worlds) -> System Admiral -> Grand Admiral. That is the whole naval chain of command, built out of one component.

Does it reproduce the roster?

Every leader role, each as one cell. 32 cells, 27 filled, 5 justified blanks - and every single cell now carries either a post title with the decision it owns, or a written justification for why no seat lives there. 26 roster roles plus the Head of State = 27 seats.

EVERY CELL IS ANSWERED - that was the missing discipline. A blank that says "commerce is empire-outward" is a hand-wave, not a justification, and it looked especially thin next to a Commerce column that does have a field seat. Working each blank properly turned three of them into real posts: Port Authority (System x Commerce), Security Chief (Planet x Espionage) and System Prefect (System x Governor). The remaining five each name the seat or the mechanic that already owns that decision. The Prefect is the one my own justification got wrong - I argued unrest is per-province so no system seat could own it, and missed that a STATION is a province too: StationFactory gives it its own legitimacy, morale and rebellion state, and the Interior Minister only covers planets. Station stability had no owner in the entire grid.
THE FIELD ROW IS NOW COMPLETE - every domain has a working end (developer: "all domains should have a field version"). The governance doc's own grid left three Field cells blank while filling Diplomacy and Espionage at that scope - an asymmetry, not a rule. Each of the three is grounded in substrate that already exists: Yard Master (IndustryAbilityDB.ProductionLines is a job queue per industrial component, so one yard already has its own sequence to run) - Convoy Master (SetLogisticsOrder + LogiBaseDB / LogiShipDB - and the governance doc explicitly promises "the player CAN take direct control of a specific hauler/convoy/route and hand-fly it while the rest stays on auto") - Magistrate (PlanetRegionsDB + CityGrid give a world real regions; per-region unrest is the missing half).

And filling the Field row exposes the real shape of the eight remaining blanks - they are not arbitrary, they are three different vertical patterns:

PatternDomainsFillsWhy
TerritorialSpace military - Planetary military - Economics - Scienceevery scopethey act on places, and a place exists at every altitude - empire, system, world, one-of
OutwardDiplomacy - Espionage - Commerceempire + field onlythey act on a relationship or a route, which has no system or planet altitude - you do not have "diplomacy with Sirius", you have diplomacy with a faction, worked by an operative
GoverningGovernorevery scopeunrest is local, but a system holds several provinces - and stations are provinces the Interior Minister cannot reach, because they are not worlds

Every remaining blank passes the roster's own test - "a distinct leader seat is justified only if it owns a decision the player would otherwise make by hand, and that neither the leader above nor below it already owns." A System-scope Ambassador would own nothing the empire's Foreign Minister or the field Ambassador does not; a Planet-scope trade seat would own nothing the Convoy Master or the Planetary Governor's stockpile lever does not.

The no-dead-weight proof is structural, not editorial. Because every seat sits at a unique (scope x domain) intersection, no two can own the same decision - the thing the governance doc demands of every role ("A distinct leader seat is justified only if it owns a decision the player would otherwise make by hand, and that neither the leader above nor below it already owns.") is enforced by the shape of the door itself.

Cradle to grave - the whole chain, on one component

This is the test that matters, and the proposal passes it end to end:

a command component is researched -> built from materials -> installed, opening a seat at some scope x domain -> an academy-trained officer is seated in it -> the player sets its stance and funding -> the delegate runs that scope at a competence cost -> the player drops in whenever they like -> the officer is lost - killed at a dangerous posting, turned by a rival, or the HQ is destroyed and the whole delegation collapses.

That last rung is why this is worth doing. Blow up an enemy's sector capital and their delegated command of that region falls apart - they revert to hand-flying or go rudderless. Command infrastructure becomes a target, and "who is holding this together?" becomes a question with teeth.

L1 is the load-bearing one. The governance doc calls seat durability "the load-bearing prerequisite under all of it" - if a seat cannot hold its officer through a combat tick, nothing above it survives either.

Three rulings owed before any of this is written. Does a ship's captain become a seat? Cleanest, but it makes a bridge component mandatory to have a captain - a real change to every existing ship design. Ambassador and Envoy - RULED: merged. The roster called them "tight but distinct." The developer's call: "they're just different stages of the same thing." Both sat at Field x Diplomacy, and once Span was cut there was nothing left to separate them. And the stage is already a dial: a low-Grade posting is an envoy sent for one negotiation; a high-Grade one is a standing ambassador at a rival's court. Same seat, bought bigger - which is exactly the reason Span had to go, applied one row further down. That took the doc roster from 21 to 20 (before the three new Field seats below took it to 23). Is AdminLevel safe to collapse? It is a [JsonProperty] enum, so saved values are ordinal - dropping the middle three shifts every value above them and breaks existing saves (landmine L3). It needs explicit numbering or a converter. Decide this one before anybody writes code.
