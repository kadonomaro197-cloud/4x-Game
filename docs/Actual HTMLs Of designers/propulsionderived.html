Pulsar4X · component designer · door 3 of 11 · complete

One door for everything that moves — reaction mass, a surface, and one FTL tab that builds any franchise.

The engine designer today gives you a size and a fuel. Thrust, exhaust velocity and burn rate are all computed and shown, not set — so a bigger engine is strictly better and there is no trade to make. This is the whole door re-derived from what the simulation actually reads, with everything built so far marked ✅.

⚠ “Spacetime” is gone, and it was my error. I had it as a fourth thing you push against, sitting beside reaction mass — but a ship carries both, so they were never alternatives. The door asks two questions: how you move inside a system, and how you leave it.

🔑 And the second question is ONE tab, not a list of drives. Warp, hyperspace, jump drives and gates are not separate components — they are three settings (route × transit × can-you-be-found) on the same part, over the same sliders. The count comes out exactly right: six settings-combinations, six science-fiction FTLs, one empty box the derivation predicts and nobody has claimed.

🔴 Follow that chain and the door empties out. Thrust is linear in engine mass, and exhaust velocity does not depend on mass at all. A bigger engine is strictly better on both counts. The most famous trade in rocketry — hard push and poor economy, or gentle push and enormous range — cannot be expressed.

So this door is the opposite problem from Weapons. Weapons had five doors and forty-one dial groups that needed collapsing to two choices and four sliders. Propulsion needs dials ADDED — and the physics for them is already half-computed.

The trade it is missing — and it runs three ways

For a given engine power, thrust × exhaust velocity is a constant

Power is ½·ṁ·v² and thrust is ṁ·v, so T·v = 2P. Spend your power on a fast thin exhaust and you get economy with little push; on a slow heavy one and you get push with little range. Real zero-sum physics, already true — it needs no invention, only exposing. It is the exact mirror of the weapon's shot-size ↔ rate-of-fire, and of the zero-sum plate tuning.

And the third leg is already wired: signature IS thrust

Sensor Signature → SensorSignatureAtb(3500 K, magnitude = Thrust). That feeds live detection — so pushing harder costs you stealth, not merely economy. Detection decides who shoots first, which makes this the richest single trade found in any category, and it already ships. Nothing in the designer tells the player.

🔒 But loud has to cost more than it does. Detection range grows as √signature — double your thrust and you are seen from only 1.41× as far, so loud is nearly free. The ruling: signature suppression is a tax on the whole power budget (P_drive = P_total × (1 − suppression)), costing push and economy at once, with the shroud's mass dragging acceleration down again. It pays twice.

And the cost already there is the largest in the game: the test suite's FirstStrike_SeerWipesBlindEnemy_Unscathed — two equal fleets, one blinded, and the seeing side wipes it taking zero losses. Signature does not cost a stat; it costs the opening exchange.

Two questions, not one — why “spacetime” had to go

The test for a door is are the answers mutually exclusive. Reaction mass and a surface pass — nothing both flies on rockets and walks. Spacetime failed it, and the game’s own ships say so. Every warship in the base mod carries a thruster and a warp drive:

🔒 So the honest reading: a door whose options can all be true at once is not a door — it is two doors wearing one label. The fix is to say what each one asks:

① Sublight — how do you move inside a system?

Reaction mass or a surface. Genuinely exclusive, and one power budget spent two ways in both cases. This is the question the door was always answering well.

② FTL method — how do you cross between systems?

One tab, three settings — route × transit × what can find you. Those three ARE the science-fiction taxonomy, and they land in a completely different box from the first question. “Spacetime” was one value of one of these settings, mis-filed as a rival to the first question.

🔑 Tall gearing is fast on roads; low gearing crawls over rough. Speed and rough-handling are the SAME zero-sum split — they were simply authored as two independent dials. That is why space thrust and planetary locomotion belong behind one door: the same budget law in different media.

The code still has two implementations of that one verb

NewtonionThrustAtb (ExhaustVelocity · FuelType · FuelBurnRate) and GroundLocomotionAtb (SpeedFactor · RoughHandling · Amphibious) share zero fields. The design already said to merge them; grouping means deleting GroundLocomotionAtb, not merging it. ⚠ That is a serialized-type removal — it breaks existing saves, so it needs a converter or a clean-save decision.

✅ The mode dial existed all along — it was just unreadable. All four frames exposed it as a raw integer, so the assembler showed “Locomotion: 1” with the meaning stuffed into a description reading '0=Foot 1=Tracked 2=Walker 3=Hover.' It is now a named picker. ⚠ One trap for the next enum dial: the renderer builds max − min entries, so the max must be the count, not the top index — leaving it at 3 would have silently cut Hover off the list.

✅ Amphibious is now a real dial. It used to charge mass and be read by nothing — ocean was hard-coded impassable to everyone. Passability is now per-unit: an amphibious drive crosses water at a steep cost (4.0 vs rough ground's 2.5 — swimming is slower than climbing a mountain, so it buys access, not speed), and it is lost with the drive below half health.

🔴 One thing still wrong here. SpeedMultForUnit returns a mounted locomotion component's SpeedFactor outright, falling back to the frame's mode only when nothing is mounted. So the moment a unit carries a designed drive, whether its frame is Legs or Hover stops affecting speed at all — the four modes are dead weight on any properly-designed unit. The mode should scale the designed factor, not be replaced by it. Behaviour change; needs a ruling and a re-baselined gauge.

❌ “Air or water” is not a family. Grepped for airbreathing, hover, buoyancy, fluid-drive, atmospheric-thrust: zero components. It is Reaction with the propellant scooped instead of carried, plus a medium requirement — the same collapse as reactionless and gravitic.

✅ Built — the fuel’s refinement level drives the engine

A fuel used to affect one number: exhaust velocity. But Thrust = Exhaust Velocity × Fuel Consumption — so a better fuel raised range and push at once. A ladder, not a choice. Each fuel now carries a grade multiplying the engine’s mass flow, set so grade × exhaust velocity is nearly constant across the chemical three: one budget, split differently.

🔑 The multiplier went on mass flow, not exhaust velocity: exhaust velocity sets Δv per kg, so scaling that would rebuild the ladder. Mass flow sets thrust and burn RATE — so a high-grade fuel pushes harder and drains the tank faster. RP-1 stopped being a dead recipe (Methalox beat it on both counts before). And it composes with signature for free: a higher-grade fuel is heard from farther. Your refinery already makes all four — every one is a refining recipe, so the cradle-to-grave chain was already whole.

🔴 Calling it a “method” exposed two things in the code

Once the question is which method rather than what you push against, you can go and check what methods the engine actually supports. The answer is one — and the one is compulsory.

① There is no way to build a ship with NO FTL drive

Three separate movement paths gate on the warp blob: JumpOrder (if (!ship.HasDataBlob<WarpAbilityDB>()) continue;), MoveToNearestAction, and MoveToSystemBodyOrder. So a hull with a thruster and no warp drive cannot be ordered anywhere at the strategic layer — including through a gate that would do the work for it.

That kills the “gate-only civilisation” build outright — B5, Stargate and Mass Effect all run on it, and it is the cheapest FTL flavour there is because the map already does the moving. It is also a Cradle-to-Grave hole: shoot a warp drive off a ship and by these three checks it should be stranded, which is a far more interesting loss than it currently reads as.

② You transit a jump point using your warp drive

Grepped for a jump drive: none exists. There is no charge time, no jump range, no cooldown, no per-jump cost — a gate transit is free and instant if you happen to own a warp drive. So of the six boxes above, only box 1 is fully built. Box 6’s network ships without a drive; boxes 2–5 are unbuilt. Every “build state” cell in that table is a grep, not a guess.

✅ Built — the warp dial, and FTL stopped being silent

Warp had one performance dial. Two live numbers had no choice in them.

Bubble creation gates departure — a ship refuses to leave while stored energy is below it. Bubble sustain is charged per second in transit. Both were computed from engine power with nothing to decide. The new slider multiplies creation and divides sustain, so their product never moves — zero-sum, the same shape as T·v = 2P.

SettingCreationSustainSignatureThe build
0.4×0.4×2.5loudestcheap to start, dear to hold — short hops, leaves on a part-charged battery
1.0×1×1—today’s numbers exactly — byte-identical
2.5×2.5×0.4quietestdear to start, cheap to hold — one long haul

The range is reciprocal-symmetric around 1.0 (1/2.5 = 0.4), so the two ends are equal and opposite.

✅ And FTL emitted nothing at all

Grepped every engine template: the two thrusters carry a sensor signature; alcubierre-warp-drive, inertialess-drive and reactionless-drive carry none. So a ship crossing a system at FTL was exactly as detectable as one parked — against a genre where warp signatures and hyperspace wakes are staples.

Fixed with one property, and it makes the dial trade three ways: the magnitude is the power you pour into holding the bubble, so it divides by the dial. The endurance drive buys range AND stealth with the same slider, and pays for both at the departure gate.

⚠ Why the band is the thruster band and not something exotic

A gravimetric signature — its own long wavelength, needing a dedicated sensor — is the richer idea, and the scan genuinely does band-match. But the only receiver in the base mod is tuned to 600 nm ± 250, so a 300 K warp signature at ~9,660 nm would be invisible to every sensor in the game — a stealth exploit, not a feature. Giving FTL its own band is a two-part change: the signature and a receiver that can see it. Flagged, not done; the gauge pins the band so nobody moves half of it.

Why this door is the game’s biggest defensive purchase

Evasion is a multiplier — there is no to-hit roll anywhere

EffToughness = Toughness ÷ landedFraction. Evasion divides into toughness, so 1 ÷ landed is an effective-health multiplier. The ground resolver writes the same arithmetic backwards. Nothing rolls a die — so “42% on target” is describing dice that do not exist.

✅ The two ceilings used to disagree — ×20 point-blank but ×50 at range, because a different dial bound each case and only one was named “cap”. They now agree at ×20 everywhere. It only ever bit low-rate-of-fire ballistics (saturation below ~2.6); flak floors itself far higher.

🔴 Evasion is still twice the multiplier armour can ever be — and it is bought at this door, in a different currency, against no defence budget. It is a matchup multiplier though: ×1 against a beam, because you cannot dodge light. So any readout must be a curve, never one number.

What collapses

Reactionless · Gravitic

Reaction with a rule removed — thrust set directly, propellant burn zero; or the medium requirement lifted. A flag, not a family.

Inertialess · Teleport

Neither is propulsion. Inertialess writes one thing — an evasion floor; it produces no thrust and moves nothing, so it is a defensive component sitting in this category because it happens to be about dodging. Teleport breaks distance rather than crossing it — a logistics mechanic.

Fluid

No component, no variable. Reaction with the propellant scooped, plus a medium requirement.

Spacetime — as a thing you push against

My own error, corrected above. A ship carries a thruster and a warp drive, so the two were never alternatives. Warp survives as one setting of the FTL tab; the word “spacetime” goes.

One component per FTL flavour

Four separate drive templates — warp, hyperspace, jump, gate-tug — would be four copies of the same four sliders with a rule changed. They collapse to one part with three settings. Exactly the collapse Weapons needed: five doors and forty-one dial groups became two choices and four sliders.

The grav surveyor ➡ Sensors

Finding a jump point is not moving through one. GravSurveyAtb is a detection instrument with a receiver, a speed and a quadratic mass — the same shape as every other sensor. It moves to Sensors ▸ Survey.

Dials that write nothing

Thrust vectoring and spool-up response (the resolver is non-positional and not per-shot-timed); reliability and ground pressure (not modelled). Drive heat is the real gap — weapons feed a fleet heat pool; propulsion feeds nothing. The asymmetry is real and the wire is small.

So the tab shows four sliders in box 1, three in box 2, and two in box 6. Every removal has a physical reason, and the panel states it as you switch:

🔴 Quietness dies under hidden transit

You are not in normal space during the crossing, so there is no signature to suppress. The only exposure left is the spool — and the split slider already decides how long that lasts. Keeping a quietness dial here would be a slider that writes nothing.

🔴 The bubble split dies on a lane — and that is a real gameplay difference

A lane already exists. There is nothing to spin up and nothing to hold, so the creation lump that WarpMoveCommand uses to gate departure simply does not apply. Which means: a warp ship with a flat battery cannot leave. A lane ship can — it just crawls. The budget splits the rocket way instead (push hard and burn a lot per AU, or sip and take longer), so the panel drops the “energy to start” tile entirely.

🔴 Three of four die on the gate coupler

Reach vs power — there is no distance to cover, so power-per-kg buys nothing. Quietness — the transit is instant, and the loud thing is the gate, not you. The bubble split — no path to make, no duration to hold. What is left is the one trade a coupler has: tonnage per transit against cycle time, with throughput fixed by drive mass. Two sliders, and that is the honest size of a part whose only job is to couple.

🔑 And every box keeps its own zero-sum. Drag the split end to end in any of the six and the product holds: start × draw · range × tempo · speed ÷ energy-per-AU · tonnage × cycles-per-hour. Four different trades, one law — the same T·v = 2P shape the reaction engine and the gearbox already run on.

Where the door stands

Two questions — how you move inside a system, and how you leave it — and then the same two dials, re-purposed by the medium. Every kind of drive is how much drive you fit × how you spend it. Reaction splits thrust ↔ exhaust velocity; Surface splits speed ↔ grip; FTL splits startup ↔ endurance, or range ↔ tempo when the transit is instant — the same two numbers under different names.

And the settings change WHICH SLIDERS EXIST, not just their labels: four in box 1, three under hidden transit (quietness has nothing to hide from), a different split on a lane (no bubble to create — so a lane ship can leave on a flat battery where a warp ship cannot), and two on the gate coupler (no distance, no duration, no path to make). Each box keeps its own zero-sum: start × draw · range × tempo · speed ÷ energy-per-AU · tonnage × cycles-per-hour — four trades, one law.
