Pulsar4X · component designer · door 1 of the remaining eight

One equation, read from five ends. Four of the dials write nothing at all.

Every sensor, cloak, jammer and survey in the game is the same inverse-square line read from a different side. Nine attributes, five jobs, and four dials that fail the only test that matters — two of them exploitable. Build one below and watch what the sim would actually read.

🔴 And one broken line underneath all of it. The band-matching gate never checks the receiver's upper edge — so the loudest thing on a ship is detected by a sensor that cannot see its wavelength, and the door's one honest trade is defeated. The game currently depends on that bug.

🔑 Not one emitter peak falls inside the receiver's window. The reactor — which is 100× louder than a thruster and therefore the number that actually sets your detection range — sits a full 1000 nm outside it.

So why does detection work at all? Because the gate is broken. SensorTools.cs tests
Math.Max(recvMin, sigMin) < Math.Max(sigMin, sigMax)
and the right-hand side is just sigMax, so the receiver's upper edge is never consulted. The test reduces to "is my band's bottom edge below the signal's top edge" — which lets through every signal at a longer wavelength than your band and blocks every signal at a shorter one. A standard overlap test would read max(recvMin, sigMin) < min(recvMax, sigMax).
And the game DEPENDS on that bug. Fix the line on its own and the reactor becomes invisible — detection range against a stock ship collapses from 0.40 Gm to 0.04 Gm, and a ship with its engines off becomes completely undetectable. That is precisely the "sat at Luna, saw nothing, no battle" failure the whole detection rebalance was written to cure. The fix has to be one change: correct the overlap test AND give the base mod an infrared receiver — which is the same ruling the FTL-band question was already waiting on.

It also means the band-centre dial has a dominant setting: tune it as short as it goes and the one-sided test passes for everything in the game. Try it in the builder above.

What this kills

🔴 Resolution — dead AND free. The worst dial found in any door.

SensorTools.cs:125 reads it into a local variable and never uses it again; that local is the only reader in the entire repository. And it is not in the mass formula, so it costs nothing either. A slider with a 0.1–1000 range that writes nothing and costs nothing. Worse than the old Amphibious dial, which at least charged mass for its silence. Do not delete it — wire it. The code comments beg for it ("resolution should be required to pick out multiple ships close together instead of just one big signal"), SignalQuality already exists, and resolution is what turns "something is out there" into "it is three destroyers."

🔴 Two fire-control dials cost mass and write nothing — a free 16× saving

Size vs Range and Size vs TrackingSpeed appear in exactly one place: the mass formula. Neither is passed to the attribute. So set both to their 0.25 minimum and fire-control mass drops to one sixteenth with no loss of range or tracking. Fix this before Chassis (door 3), which is about to build its entire budget on a currency that can be cheated.

🔴 The jammer's downside is player-optional, so it is not a trade

Its own docstring states the catch: "blind them, and paint a target on yourself." But Self Signature Boost is a dial (1–10) and it is not in the mass formula. So a player sets it to 1 and gets a jammer with no beacon penalty at all. 🔒 A penalty you can dial away for free is not a cost, it is decoration. The coupling is obvious and physical — a barrage jammer's noise IS its self-signature — so they should be one number, not two dials.

🔴 Scan interval is a free ladder

It writes something real (the reschedule interval), but for a sensor it costs nothing — no mass, no power. The only energy path is the solar-array branch, which is not a sensor. So shorter is strictly better and every player pins it at one second. Give it a power draw and sweep-often-and-run-hot vs sweep-rarely-and-stay-cold becomes a real EMCON decision that composes with the rest of the detection game.

🔴 And antenna size is a free ladder too — I had this one wrong

I first wrote that this dial was already honest, because range goes as antenna size and mass as its square, so range ∝ √mass. The arithmetic says otherwise. Mass is 90 + 0.01 × size², and the quadratic term does not overtake the 90 kg constant until size ≈ 95 — while the shipped sensor sits at 1.25. So across the dial's entire usable range the mass is effectively flat: size 10 buys 8× the detection range for +1 kg. The cost law is right in principle and mis-scaled by two orders of magnitude in practice.

The one honest trade — and the bug takes it away

Bandwidth is a genuine zero-sum, and it is exact

Bandwidth writes two things: the width of your window, and the efficiency term inside your sensitivity. Work it through and coverage × range² is a constant — verified numerically at 39.34 across the whole dial:

🔑 A narrow window hears a whisper from far away; a wide one hears many kinds of thing but only close by. That is a real decision, it already ships, and the designer presents it as two unrelated numbers.

🔴 Except the gate bug cancels it. If your band's lower edge only has to sit below the signal's upper edge, then a 1 nm window still detects everything in the game — so you take the 6.27 Gm and give up nothing. The one honest trade in this door is only honest once the overlap test is fixed. Two findings, one line of code.

Where the door stands

One choice — what this part does with the spectrum — and the choice decides which sliders exist. Five jobs (Listen · Look · Track · Hide · Blind), one inverse-square equation read from five ends, and a clean split into what you can see × what you emit.

🔴 Five dials fail the only test that matters (does it write a sim variable, and does it cost something): resolution is dead and free · two fire-control dials cost mass and write nothing, for a free 16× saving · the jammer's self-noise is opt-out · the scan interval is free · and antenna size is free across its whole usable range. Two of those are exploits, not omissions.

🔴 And one broken line sits under everything: the band-matching gate never checks the receiver's upper edge, so the loudest emitter aboard a ship is detected by a sensor a thousand nanometres off its wavelength — and the door's one genuine trade (coverage ↔ reach, exact to 39.34) is cancelled, because a 1 nm window still sees everything. The game depends on the bug: fix the line alone and a parked ship becomes undetectable.

Yours to call: ① the band ruling — ✅ the overlap-test half is now BUILT, flag-gated (EnableBandMatchFix, default OFF, gauge BandMatchTests); the remaining half is to add an infrared receiver to the base-mod loadout and flip the flag together (the atomic go-live), or compute the band and remove the dial. This is the same ruling the deferred FTL-band question was waiting on, so one decision closes both. ② one cost law for reach, or two — ground radar prices it linearly, space quadratically. ③ whether to wire resolution (contact fidelity becomes a purchase) or cut it.
