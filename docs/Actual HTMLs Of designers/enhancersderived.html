Pulsar4X - component designer - door 6 of the remaining eight

Eight templates that make other things better - and on six of them, you set your own price.

A veteran cadre, an automation suite, power armour, a shield generator, a ward projector, reflex boosters, a training cadre, sealed systems. Every one of them is the same component: pick what it improves, pick by how much. One choice, one slider. It reproduces all eight.

And six of the eight have their mass on a SEPARATE dial from their capability. Mass = PropertyValue('CarryMass') - so the weight is a number you type in, independent of what the part actually does. Set the mass to its minimum and the capability to its maximum and you get a x2 firepower multiplier for a tonne, total evasion for a kilogram, 200 crew saved for nothing.

One of them is worse than free - it is a bootstrap. Power armour's StrengthBonus adds to the carry budget while its CarryMass is what it spends from it. Both are dials. So a one-kilogram part can grant +2,999 carry capacity, which you then spend on more of them. The ground unit assembler's budget does not have a ceiling. And the engine's own error message, when you run out of room, is: "Add an augment (e.g. power armour) or use a bigger frame."

Both fixes are already written in the codebase. Two of the eight templates price their capability into their mass formula - and one of them says so out loud: "Training is EARNED: the higher you dial the multiplier, the steeper the mass." And the exact leverage cap the augments need is already running one attribute over, on the ground weapons.

Better at carrying

Raises the frame's carry budget so an augmented human can lug a weapon a bare human cannot - the Space Marine story, and a genuinely good mechanic. It is also the one that bootstraps.

Better at running with fewer people

Crew Reduction

crew-automation cuts a hull's bulk crew requirement - spending mass and research instead of scarce workforce (a real pool, via ManpowerTools). Its own description says exactly that. The dial is free.

So the collapse is: one choice - what does it improve? - and one slider, by how much. The four ground-augment templates (power-armor, shield-generator, ward-projector, reflex-booster) are literally the same attribute with different defaults: all four carry all five dials, and each simply leaves the four it does not use at zero. A shield generator ships with StrengthBonus 0..1000 it never uses; a reflex booster ships with Shield 0..1000 it never uses. They are one component, copied four times.

The same enhancer, built for ships and for ground troops - and only the ground one charges

These two components do the identical job: embed a veteran cadre so an identical chassis fights above its class. Compare their Mass formulas.

SHIP - unit-caliber "Veteran Cadre"
Mass = PropertyValue('Cadre Mass') - dials: Cadre Mass 1,000-20,000 - Firepower Caliber 1.0-2.0 - Toughness Caliber 1.0-2.0
=> neither caliber appears in any cost formula. Cadre Mass 1,000 + both calibers 2.0 = a x2/x2 combat multiplier for one tonne.
GROUND - ground-training-cadre "Training Cadre"
Mass = 50 x (1 + (TrainingMultiplier - 1) x 4) - dial: TrainingMultiplier 1.0-2.0
=> the multiplier IS the mass. Dialing 1.0 -> 2.0 takes the part from 50 kg to 250 kg, and research and credits follow.

And the ground template says so in its own description: "Training is EARNED: the higher you dial the multiplier, the steeper the mass/credits/research/build-time to field the unit. Multiplier range + cost curve are FLAGGED balance values." Somebody understood the problem exactly, wrote the fix, flagged their own numbers for review - and the ship version, which shipped in the same era, does not have it.

Read the bottom-left cell again. The most powerful ship cadre you can design is cheaper than the one that ships - because the shipped design pays 3,000 kg of Cadre Mass for a x1.3, and the free version pays 1,000 kg for a x2.0. The design in the game is strictly worse than the design a player will make in thirty seconds, and the difference is a x1.54 firepower advantage on every warship in the fleet, for a third of the mass. That is the largest free combat multiplier found anywhere in the designer.
The fix is a transcription, exactly like Power's was. Mass = Cadre Mass x (1 + (Firepower Caliber - 1) x k + (Toughness Caliber - 1) x k), with k chosen so the shipped 3,000 kg / x1.30 / x1.20 design is byte-identical. The ground template already demonstrates the shape and already flags its own coefficient as a balance value. Nothing here needs designing.

Power armour is not a leak - it is a bootstrap

This is the most serious single finding in the campaign, and it needs three lines of code read side by side. From GroundUnitAssembly.Compute:

pass 1 - augments raise the budget
capacity += d.GetAttribute<GroundAugmentAtb>.StrengthBonus * c;
r.MaxItemWeight = capacity * MaxItemFraction; MaxItemFraction = 0.5

pass 2 - and what they cost is a different dial
itemMass = g.Mass; which is Mass = PropertyValue('CarryMass')
used += itemMass * c;

the gate
if (used > capacity)...invalid

StrengthBonus and CarryMass are two independent dials on the same part. One adds to the budget; the other spends from it. Neither constrains the other. So the net contribution of a power-armour design is StrengthBonus - CarryMass, and you choose both.

A power-armour designCarryMass what it spendsStrengthBonus what it grantsNet to the budgetHeaviest single part now allowed
the shipped Power Armour30300+270185
the same, mass dialled down1300+299199
both dials at their extremes13,000+2,9991,549
ten of those1030,000+29,99015,045 a human frame now carries a siege railgun
So the ground unit assembler's carry budget has no ceiling - and because MaxItemWeight = capacity x 0.5, the per-item cap rises with it too. A 20 kg human frame with a base carry strength of 100 can be walked up to tens of thousands of carry capacity using parts that each weigh one unit. Every gate that depends on the carry budget - the per-item limit, the power-supply gate, the ammo gate - is downstream of a number the player can inflate at will. This is the ground-side equivalent of the Chassis door's free mass budget, except it compounds.
And for the third time in this campaign, the engine's own error message advises pulling the free lever. GroundUnitAssembly.cs:226: "{name} (carry-mass 300) is too heavy for this frame - max single item is 50. Add an augment (e.g. power armour) or use a bigger frame." Friendly, accurate, and it is telling the player how to bypass the budget. The building foundation says "raise the Footprint Budget"; the station says "raise the structure budget". Three helpful messages, three free levers. A good error message is evidence about what the designer expected to be costly.
And the cap this needs is already running, one attribute over, on the ground weapons. When the Attack dial turned out to be free, the fix was a floor on the part's carry-mass driven by its capability:
itemMass = Math.Max(w.Mass, w.Attack * AttackCarryFactor); AttackCarryFactor = 0.1
Apply the identical pattern to the augments - Math.Max(CarryMass, StrengthBonus * 0.1) - and the shipped Power Armour is byte-identical (300 x 0.1 = 30, exactly its authored CarryMass), while the leverage is capped at a stated, uniform 10:1. Power armour still lets you carry ten times what it weighs, which is what power armour is for. It just stops being infinite. The pattern, the coefficient and the byte-identity anchor are all already in the file.

The designer - build one enhancer

This is the one place you design an enhancer: one door, one template, two sliders - and it reproduces all eight shipped templates, each opening on its real shipped part. Tick the box to price the capability into the mass, the way two of the eight already do. Below the designer is a read-only map of what this door cannot build yet - the frontier, not a second builder.

The first door - what KIND of better?

The three locked doors of 6, and they split by what you are upgrading. Augmentation raises the host's physical envelope - carry, evasion, protection - whatever the frame is. Advanced Training stamps an extra layer of buff onto the unit at design time - it is simply better than the baseline. Systems lets the machine do a crew or yard job - automation, a battle-management link, self-repair. Environmental thresholds and damage control are NOT here - they are Defense > Hardening 5.3, and they moved. See.

The second door - which enhancer?

These are TEMPLATES, not dials. Each row below is a thing you can build; the dials are the numbers you set on it, and 6.0 locks the same five for every enhancer: what it boosts - magnitude - host - cost/side-effect - grave rung. So "Magnitude" is a dial that every template has - not a door, which is what an earlier pass here got wrong. The sliders under are this template's instance of those dials.

Five templates used to be on this row and are not any more. sealed-systems, pressure, radiation, damage-control and redundancy are Defense > Hardening 5.3 - "surviving the environment, not the weapon". They moved rather than being deleted; see the Defense door.

The forced choice: it picks which template you are building and therefore which single number the enhancer writes into the simulation. All seven ask the same question - how much better does this make the thing? - and five of them charge nothing for the answer. The two marked are the reference implementations that do.

The other two free dials, and the one that is quietly the most expensive

Crew Reduction - 200 people saved, for nothing

crew-automation: Mass = PropertyValue('Automation Mass'), and Crew Reduction (0 -> 200) is a separate dial that appears in no cost formula. So a 1,000 kg suite can cut 200 crew off a hull. And the template's own description explains precisely why that matters: "It cuts the BULK crew requirement, letting you spend mass and high tech instead of scarce workforce." Crew is a scarce pool - ManpowerTools.ResolveBuild gates construction on it, and a government's CrewPolicy decides whether a shortage blocks the build or conscripts. So this dial converts a scarce strategic resource into nothing, and the template knows the resource is scarce. Same fix: put Crew Reduction into the mass formula.

EvasionBonus - total evasion for one kilogram, on the best defence in the game

reflex-booster: CarryMass 1 -> 2,000 and EvasionBonus 0 -> 1.0, independent. Evasion 1.0 is every attack missing. And the Defense door already put a number on how much that is worth: evasion is an effective-health multiplier up to x20, where armour reaches x10. So the single most valuable defensive stat in the game is on a free dial, on a part whose weight you also choose. One kilogram of reflex wetware makes a unit unkillable by aimed fire. (Saturation fire still floors it - the weapon triangle holds - which is the only reason this is not the worst finding in the door.)

Shield and ShieldRegenFraction - a 1,500-point pool, or a 600-point pool that refills five times faster

Same shape: shield-generator can hold 1,500 shield for one carry-mass; ward-projector can hold 600 and recharge at ShieldRegenFraction 5.0, also free. And the ward projector's own description is a model of good design writing - it explains the capacity-versus-recharge trade in plain terms ("a smaller pool that recharges much faster between salvos... so it shrugs off sustained chip-fire where a big slow shield eats one alpha then stays down") and flags both dials for a balance pass. The trade it describes is real and it is the right trade. It just is not priced, so both ends are free and the trade never bites.

sealed-systems - the second template that already charges, and it is the cheapest one to copy

Mass = 40 x (1 + Sealing x 1.5). One dial, in the mass formula, so a fully sealed suit (Sealing 1.0) weighs 100 kg against an unsealed 40 kg - and research and credits follow from mass. It also, like the training cadre, flags its own coefficient: "Sealing fraction + cost curve are FLAGGED balance values." Two templates in this door were written by someone applying exactly the rule this whole campaign has been deriving, and they left a note saying which numbers they were unsure about. That is the standard.

Four templates, one attribute - the collapse in the data

The four ground augments are not four components. They are one component with four sets of defaults, and every one of them carries every dial:

Every grey cell is a live dial with a real range that the template leaves at zero. So a player designing a "shield generator" can set its StrengthBonus to 1,000 and its EvasionBonus to 1.0 - it is the same attribute, and nothing forbids it. Which means the four templates are not four choices; they are four suggestions. That is not necessarily wrong - the developer's rule is "anyone can create anything within reason" - but it does mean the door already IS one component with one slider per axis, and the four templates are just presets. Naming them as presets rather than as separate parts would make the designer honest about what it is.

And this is where the door's one genuinely good structural decision lives. GroundUnitAssembly handles stacking correctly and deliberately: shields and evasion ADD across parts (mount two wards, get both pools), while training and sealing take the BEST and do not stack (if (t.TrainingMultiplier > bestTraining) - "the best cadre's multiplier wins; they don't stack"). That distinction is exactly right - you can wear two shields but you cannot be trained twice - and it is documented at both sites. Nothing to change.

And here the "oldest attribute" lesson finally has a clean counter-example, which sharpens it. Sensors said look at the oldest; Chassis added new code gets the structure right and forgets the economy. This door breaks both: unit-caliber and crew-automation are recent (the 6.2 / 6.3 slices, with their own CI gauges) and they are the ones that forgot to charge - while ground-training-cadre and sealed-systems, written in the same month, price their dials and annotate their own uncertainty. So age is not the predictor. What predicts it is whether the author was thinking about the COST or about the CAPABILITY - and the two who got it right both left a "FLAGGED balance values" note, which is what thinking about cost looks like in a diff.

THE LIMIT - what makes a Space Marine, a Jedi, and a ship that survives a season

CORRECTION - this section once proposed Threshold and Endurance as new Enhancer doors. They are neither new nor Enhancers. A read of docs/economy/COMPONENT-DESIGNER-DIALS.md (3,807 lines, all 11 categories / 37 doors LOCKED) found both already designed and homed: Threshold is Defense > Hardening 5.3 dials A+B - "surviving the environment, not the weapon... Hardening buys operation where an unhardened hull degrades or fails." Damage-control / redundancy is Hardening 5.3 dial C verbatim. Only self-repair is Enhancers, at 6.3 Systems, and it is half of hole H2. And the wall is the same one: both halves of H2 are blocked on the parked degraded-condition / per-component-damage model - which is what "whole-or-dead" means. The analysis below stands; the homing did not. The five templates have moved to Defense.

Push this door as far as it goes and it hits a wall that is not about tuning. Every one of the seven enhancers is the same KIND of thing: a scalar multiplier on a stat the unit already has. More firepower, more toughness, more carry, more evasion, more shield, less crew, more sealing.

Nothing that makes a unit iconic in fiction is a scalar multiplier. You cannot make a Space Marine by multiplying a guardsman.

Why the two templates that already charge are both THRESHOLDS

The door has exactly two templates that price their dial: ground-training-cadre and sealed-systems. The second is a threshold - its Sealing dial decides whether the unit can operate somewhere at all. A threshold is easy to price because it is obviously worth something; a multiplier is easy to leave free because it never feels like a purchase. That, not biology, is why five of the eight are free.

One shape this door will NOT use: the forced prerequisite. An earlier pass modelled the interface as a Black Carapace - gear that makes other gear usable, so that destroying it bricks the armour. That was retired: enhancers must never be forcibly dependent on each other, or the designer becomes a build-order puzzle instead of a set of choices. The interface now stands alone in Systems - it shortens the doctrine/stance switch cooldown, and losing it just returns the unit to the base timer.

The door's honest edge: one kind is built (Magnitude, in the designer above), two moved to Defense (Threshold, and Endurance's damage-control), one points at Sensors (Foresight), and one is an emergent readout rather than a dial (Learning). The self-repair half of Endurance is the only unbuilt piece that is genuinely this door's to build - and it waits on the same whole-or-dead ruling every hero-ship-shaped capability waits on.

THE RULE THIS PRODUCES - and it explains why iconic units feel iconic. A magnitude enhancer can always be out-scaled by simply building a bigger unit. A threshold cannot: no amount of firepower lets an unsealed trooper fight on an airless moon, and no amount of armour lets a ship act on a shot that has not been fired. Iconic units own a CATEGORY, not a number - and a category is something the enemy cannot buy their way past. That is the difference between "elite infantry" and Space Marines.

Cradle to grave - the same chain, and it still holds

None of these break the rule that a capability must be reachable through the whole chain. Each is still a component: researched, built from materials, installed on a unit, and lost when that component is destroyed. The interface makes this concrete - destroy it and the unit loses its tempo edge, re-tasking at the base 300s again while the enemy who kept theirs switches stance in a third of the time. That is a grave rung with real teeth, and it needs no forced dependency to bite - a lost capability is loss enough.

One is NOT a dial, and the intrinsic test is what catches it. Learning fails 1a: you cannot set a unit's veterancy knowing only the component, because it depends on what that unit has survived. So the dial is how fast it learns and how high it can climb - the accumulated veterancy is an emergent readout, exactly like delta-V or engagement range. Build the dial; display the total; never let the designer set it directly.
