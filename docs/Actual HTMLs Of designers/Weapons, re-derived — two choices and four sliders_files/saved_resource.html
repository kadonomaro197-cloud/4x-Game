Weapons, re-derived — two choices and four sliders

Pulsar4X · component designer · re-derived from the resolver

Ten numbers reach the fight. Everything else is cost or flavour.

Five weapon doors and forty-one dial groups collapse to two choices and four sliders — because the combat math only ever reads ten values off a weapon. Build one below and watch all ten move.

The ten, as five questions

Grouped by what they are actually deciding.

How hard does it hit?

Damage per second

Which is damage per shot × rate of fire — set either and the other follows.

Does it land?

Shot speedFollows a dodgeVolume of fire

Three ways to beat a dodge: too fast to avoid, it steers after you, or so many coming that some land regardless. The math takes whichever of the first two is better, then puts a floor under it for volume.

What gets through?

Good againstArmour piercedDamage per shot

What it’s good against is the shield answer — it decides how much a shield soaks, from all of it to none. Armour pierced cancels plate point for point. Damage per shot decides whether many small hits bounce where one big hit punches through.

How far?

Range

How far it can hit from — and the longest reach in the fight sets how big the battle is.

Can it keep it up?

Heat/secSupplyInterceptable

Heat slows you down when you fire flat out. How it gets there decides whether you burn ammo or power — and whether point-defence gets a chance to shoot it down on the way in.

The asymmetry is the point. Against a shield the numbers are fixed — you decide them when you pick the setting, and they are the same on every target in the game. Against armour there is no fixed number, because the defender chose it: plating carries its own four resistances, so ablative plate can shrug off energy and thin against slugs. And past both, a hull is a hull — damage is damage.

Which means Exotic is the only setting that ignores a shield outright, and Energy is the practical middle — half of it bleeds through, so it still hurts a shielded target where a kinetic round accomplishes nothing at all.

And armour cares about shot size, shields do not. Armour subtracts a flat amount from each shot, so a hail of small shots is nearly all absorbed while one big shot mostly gets through. A shield soaks the same fraction either way. That is why the shot-size slider changes the “Armoured” number in the builder and never touches “Shielded.”

What this kills

Pulse vs Continuous Beam

A pulse of light travels at light speed too. There is no number that makes one dodgeable and the other not — the distinction was fiction. They are one weapon at two shot-size positions.

The Exotic door

Exotic is a setting on what it’s good against, not a way of getting there. A gravitic beam is Beam + Exotic; a warp torpedo is Guided + Exotic. And the non-damage parts — mind control, jump inhibition — write none of the ten. They are not weapons; calling them a weapon door hid that.

Four of the six old delivery types

Beam · Bolt · Slug · Cloud · Guided · Blast. The engine only ever asks them one question: can point-defence shoot this down on the way in? Bolt, Slug and Cloud all answer no, identically — three names for one row.

The other ten categories

Not yet re-derived — this is the locked spec as authored, 507 options. Same treatment is owed to each.

2.1REACTION4 dials · 27 opts

The Newtonian main-drive: throw reaction mass out the back, get pushed forward — everything from a chemical booster to a fusion torch to an ion cruiser falls out of these dials. This is the drive the game's physics already runs on (NewtonionThrustAtb: Thrust = ExhaustVelocity × FuelBurnRate; reach = Tsiolkovsky Ve × ln(wet/dry)), so every dial here is Modelled today — no wiring, no deferral.

Exotic (antimatter/He-3)

The extensibility slot — reactionless drives, inertialess drives, gravity manipulation, the physics-breakers. Like Weapons ▸ Exotic, this door grows as new mechanics land; today it's the home for "a drive that violates a rule the other doors pay," and it's mostly a set of named deferrals — each impossible drive escapes one constraint, and each names the prerequisite mechanic it waits on, so the physics-breakers are designed-in, not bolted-on.

The eyes: passive and active sensors that build the contact picture — who's out there, where, how far. Everything from a cheap thermal eyeball to a deep-space active array to a stealth-hunting multi-band suite falls out of these dials. The engine already runs the whole contact/attenuation/threshold model, so this door is overwhelmingly Modelled — it exposes dials onto a live system that already gates combat.

A. Sensitivity — how faint a signal it catches (the reach dial)

Coarse (high threshold)
cheap, light, low power
short range — only sees loud/close targets
Sensitive (low threshold)
sees faint + far (fainter BestSensitivity_kW → longer DetectionRange_m)
big, power-hungry, costly; a huge dish is heavy

B. Waveform band — WHAT part of the spectrum it sees

Narrow / tuned
deep in one band — sees a specific signature (heat) very far
blind to everything outside the band (a thermal eye misses a cold hull)
Wide / multi-band
catches many signature types — a stealth hull hiding in one band shows in another
shallower in each — master of none, more mass/cost

C. Active ↔ passive — ping or listen (the EMCON decision)

Passive (listen)
silent — you see without being seen; the ambush/scout posture
only catches emitters (running/firing targets); a dark drifting hull is invisible
Active (ping)
sees cold/stealthed targets via their reflection (the only way to find a dark hull)
lights you up (ActivityMultiplier ↑) — seen from ~2× your own range; you find them AND announce yourself

D. Refresh rate — how fresh the picture is (ScanTime)

Fast sweep
fresh track — catches fast movers, tight firing solution
more power/heat; a rapidly-pinging active is even louder
Slow sweep
cheaper, can integrate for more range
stale track — a fast target has moved since the last look

Low-res
a blip — "something's there"
