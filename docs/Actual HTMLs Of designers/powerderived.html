Pulsar4X · component designer · door 2 of the remaining eight

The trade this door needs was already in the codebase — and two of the three generators were options nobody would ever pick.

Smallest door on the list: three attributes, five templates. Work out the arithmetic and they are not three variations on one law — the RTG already trades output against endurance, the reactor is bare 50 × Mass, and the turbine's one dial was named after a trade it never had. So the law this door needs is copied, not invented.

✅ And then the harder question — why would anyone pick a given one? Measured on the axes the sim actually reads, two of the five power sources were beaten on every single one. The reactor asked for crew equal to its kilograms (1,500 crew for a 1,500 kg plant), which made the densest generator in the game unusable; the RTG was ~22,000× worse per kilogram than a turbine to save two crew. Both fixed, and the rule is now a test that fails if it ever happens again.

✅ FIXED — fuel is real now. No power source used to need fuel to run: all three burn-generators were charged fissile fuel to build, carried a load, and burned it down every tick, but LocalFuel was never read as a gate anywhere, so it ran negative and every reactor ran forever on nothing. The gate is wired — and “make lifetime longer” turned out to be a unit bug: the reactor's real endurance was 2.43 hours and the RTG's was 5 seconds. And the burn rate is a dial now.

🔴 And a colony cannot build a power plant at all — not one of the five templates mounts on a colony, while the code that reads a colony's power supply is already written and waiting.

What this kills

⚠️ Lifetime — CORRECTED. It costs fuel; it just costs no mass.

I checked the Mass formula and stopped there. A template prices a dial through seven independent channels — Mass · Volume · CrewReq · ResearchCost · CreditCost · BuildPointCost · ResourceCost — and the reactor's ResourceCost charges it directly: fissile-fuels = Fuel Consumption × 3600 × Lifetime. A ten-year reactor costs ten times the fissile fuel to build. The dial is paid for.

What is still wrong is narrower and still real: it adds no mass. A reactor carrying ten years of fuel weighs exactly what a one-hour reactor weighs — fuel with no weight — and mass is the currency the Chassis door gates on, so the free side of the dial is the side that matters for ship design. The RTG gets this right by construction (Fuel = Mass × 0.5); the reactor and the turbine do not. 🔒 The rule: the question is not “is it in the mass formula” but “which of the seven channels does it appear in, and is that the one the player is actually constrained by?”

⚠️ Output vs Efficency — RULED. Only the NAME was wrong. Renamed Core vs Generator.

Two corrections in a row on this one dial, and the second was yours. First correction — GennyMass: I said it was computed and never read. It is read, in the ResourceCost block, heavily — stainless-steel, copper, aluminium, nickel and tungsten all scale with it. So the split decides your bill of materials: generator-heavy eats tungsten and nickel, core-heavy eats graphite and fissile fuel. Second correction — the fixed duration: I called 12.7-years-whatever-you-set a defect. Your ruling: it is the point. A plant you fuel once a decade is the turbine's whole character, and re-purposing the dial into an output↔endurance split would have thrown that away to satisfy a naming complaint.

So the fix was one word, not one mechanic. The dial is now called what it does. 🔒 Two standing rules out of it: a dial's NAME is not evidence that a trade exists — and a wrong name is not evidence that the mechanic is wrong. Rename before you re-purpose.

🔴 A reactor is silently also a battery, and the units do not match

EnergyGenerationAtb.cs:65,70 — genDB.EnergyStoreMax[type] += PowerOutputMax. PowerOutputMax is kW; EnergyStoreMax is kJ. So installing a reactor adds storage equal to exactly one second of its own output, by dimensional accident. Two consequences: the battery bank is partly redundant, and any future balance pass on storage will be fighting a hidden term. Make it deliberate (a stated buffer, output × someSeconds) or remove it.

🔑 The solar array runs on the SENSOR code — so one ruling now touches three doors

solarArray builds an EnergySolarGenerationAtb whose constructor is the same waveform + best/worst-efficiency shape as SensorReceiverAtb — which itself carries an IsEnergyGen flag and a dedicated overload. Absorption is band-matched exactly the way detection is.

Best Efficiency = tech-panel-efficiency × (tech-panel-bandwidth × 0.5 ÷ Bandwidth)
Narrower band, better panel — the same coverage ↔ efficiency zero-sum as the sensor's bandwidth dial. The seventh place one law turns up.
⚠ Which means the Sensors ruling reaches into Power. Correcting the sensor's band-overlap test and adding an infrared receiver changes solar output too — panels and sensors read the spectrum through the same code. One decision now touches Sensors, Power, and the deferred FTL band. Worth knowing before it is made, not after.
