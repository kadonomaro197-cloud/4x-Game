🔒 "Any size" — and the decision it forces

A Death Star is an average station to one faction and an outpost to another. Two consequences, and the second one is the real work.

Consequence 1 — absolute size classes are gone

There is no "Light hull" and no "Heavy hull" as a thing. There is a continuous size slider and a name whose scale word is computed relative to what your faction already builds. The same 10 million tonnes reads Colossal to a young faction and Light to an old one. The shipped light/medium/heavy hulls become three saved presets, not three template tiers — which is also what makes them survivable as data: nothing breaks, they just stop being the only options.

Consequence 2 — the budget EXPONENT becomes a real ruling

The shipped hulls follow budget = 1128 × size0.5 (light and heavy to within 1%). That was fitted across a 50× range. Your ruling asks it to hold across fifteen orders of magnitude, and the exponent decides whether that is possible:

✅ And the answer that falls out is the one you described, arrived at from the arithmetic. Keep √ — the data votes for it — and make structural efficiency a research axis with an enormous range. Then:

At the shipped efficiency of 1,128, the biggest thing you can hold together is about 1.3 × 1010 kg — a large asteroid station. At 100,000 it is 1014 kg. To make a genuine planet-killer feasible you need roughly 107 — about nine thousand times the shipped figure, which is a whole tech tree, not a slider nudge. 🔑 So "a Death Star is average to one faction and impossible to another" is not a special case to code — it IS the efficiency gap between them. Megastructures are gated by materials science, and the gate is a curve rather than a wall: every point of efficiency makes the next order of magnitude a little less absurd.

⚠ Flagged honestly: the √ law is measured over a 50× range and extrapolated over fifteen orders of magnitude. That is a real leap and the page should say so rather than hide it. An exponent that failed the other way would need a cap, and a cap is what your ruling forbids.
