Ten numbers reach the fight. Everything else is cost or flavour.

Five weapon doors and forty-one dial groups collapse to two choices and four sliders — because the combat math only ever reads ten values off a weapon. The rest is naming, cost and description. Pick how it gets there and what it is good against; then set how hard, how big each shot, how far, how concentrated. Build one below and watch all ten values move.

This is a derived door, not an invented one: every dial writes a variable the resolver already reads, and the two choices × four sliders reproduce all eleven weapons the engine builds today — and reach roughly a thousand more the current template pile never named.

The ten, as five questions

Grouped by what they are actually deciding. Every slider and both doors below write one or more of these — and the resolver reads nothing else off the weapon.

Build one

Two choices, four sliders. The readout is the ten values the resolver would actually read — teal is a number you set, grey is one the door forced. The panel also names the shipped weapon your current settings reproduce.

Door 1 — how it gets there

Physics, not taste. Contact — nothing crosses the gap, so nothing supplies it (you must close). Beam — light speed, undodgeable, runs on power. Projectile — finite speed, a nimble target can juke it, runs on ammo. Guided — slow but it steers, runs on ammo, and is the only family point-defence can answer.

Axis 2 — what it’s good against

Which defence it defeats — and which one stops it cold. Independent of axis 1, so four by four is sixteen real weapons before you touch a slider.

The asymmetry is the point. Against a shield the numbers are fixed — you decide them when you pick the setting, and they are the same on every target in the game. Against armour there is no fixed number, because the defender chose it: plating carries its own four resistances, so ablative plate can shrug off energy and thin against slugs. And past both, a hull is a hull — damage is damage, and nobody decides it.

Which means Exotic is the only setting that ignores a shield outright, and Energy is the practical middle — half of it bleeds through, so it still hurts a shielded target where a kinetic round accomplishes nothing at all.

And armour cares about shot size, shields do not. Armour subtracts a flat amount from each shot, so a hail of small shots is nearly all absorbed while one big shot mostly gets through. A shield soaks the same fraction either way. That is why the shot-size slider changes the “Armoured” number in the builder and never touches “Shielded.” It is also why a railgun and a flak battery differ though they share a family and a dps budget.

What this kills

Pulse vs Continuous Beam

A pulse of light travels at light speed too. There is no number that makes one dodgeable and the other not — the distinction was fiction. They are one weapon at two shot-size positions.

The Exotic door

Exotic is a setting on what it’s good against, not a way of getting there. A gravitic beam is Beam + Exotic; a warp torpedo is Guided + Exotic. And the non-damage parts — mind control, jump inhibition — write none of the ten. They are not weapons; calling them a weapon door hid that.

Four of the six old delivery types

Beam · Bolt · Slug · Cloud · Guided · Blast. The engine only ever asks them one question: can point-defence shoot this down on the way in? Bolt, Slug and Cloud all answer no, identically — three names for one row.

The collapse is honest because it is derived. Every door and slider here writes a variable the resolver already reads — DamagePerSecond, DamagePerShot, Rate, VolumeOfFire, Range_m, Penetration, the shield soak, shot speed, dodge-following and runs-on. A dial that wrote nothing would be a bug in the design; there are none here.

Status

Weapons: re-derived above — 5 doors and 41 dial groups become 2 choices and 4 sliders. This is a proposal that reproduces every existing gun, not yet the shipped designer.

The template pile that this replaces grew 67 → 89 → 96 — expanding exactly where the derivation says it should shrink. Two intrinsic tests gate every dial: which sim variable does it write, and can it be set knowing only the component (if not, it is an assembly decision or an emergent readout, never a dial).
