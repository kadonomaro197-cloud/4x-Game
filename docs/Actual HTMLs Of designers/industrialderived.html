Pulsar4X - component designer - door 7 of the remaining eight

The healthiest door in the designer - which is what makes its three exceptions readable.

Mines, refineries, factories, shipyards, construction services, launch pads, field constructors, combat-engineer kits. Ten templates, one shape: output is proportional to size, and size is what you pay for. Seven of the ten get that exactly right - a bigger mine produces more and costs more, in step, so there is no dominant setting and no free win. After six doors of leaks, that is worth saying plainly.

And the three that fail all fail the same way, which finally names the rule. research-lab, bunker and infrastructure have a hard-coded number in their Mass formula - and because all seven cost channels are computed from [Mass], that single fact makes every dial on those templates free at a stroke. A 100-point research lab costs exactly what a 1-point lab costs.

Plus two dials that are simply dead. The factory's Fighter Construction Points is the only adjustable dial it presents besides Size, and it is not in the dictionary that becomes the industry rate - nor is there any fighter-construction industry type for it to feed. And the research lab's Cost Per Day is stored on the attribute and read by nothing in the codebase.

The rule this door confirms, and it is mechanically checkable: a dial is free exactly when the template's Mass formula does not read it. Two shapes - Mass = a constant, or Mass = a different dial. Every leak in all seven doors so far is one of those two. That is a CI test, not a judgement call.

Each wins an axis outright, so neither is clutter - which is exactly the rule the Power door produced (every option behind a door must win at least one axis), satisfied here without anyone having to fix anything. Mass = 100000 - and with one constant, every dial on the research lab goes free

The research lab is the most consequential template in this door: research is what unlocks every other door. Here is its entire cost structure.

So a 100-point lab and a 1-point lab are the same building. 100 tonnes, 20 crew, 120 credits, 10 research to unlock, 50,000 build points - identical at either end. A hundredfold difference in the rate at which your empire advances, for the same price. Nobody would ever build anything but a 100-point lab, which means the dial is not a decision; it is a formality with a default that punishes anyone who leaves it alone. And the shipped design leaves it at 10.
Cost Per Day is dead. ResearchPointsAtbDB stores _costPerDay with a public getter, and nothing in the engine or the client reads it. Grepping .CostPerDay finds three consumers and all three are on different classes - ResearcherDB and AdministratorDB, which model a scientist's funding, not a lab's running cost. So the lab has an upkeep field that never bills anybody, and a 0-100,000 slider in front of it. That is the second confirmed dead attribute field in the designer, after LogiBaseAtb.
The fix is the door's own pattern, applied to the one template that missed it. Mass = Research Points x k - the same shape as the refinery (Mass = Size, output Size x 0.1) and the factory (Mass = Size x 1000). Anchor k on the shipped 10-point / 100-tonne lab and it is byte-identical: k = 10,000. Then a 100-point lab weighs a thousand tonnes, needs proportional crew, and is a real investment. And decide Cost Per Day: bill it as the lab's operating cost - which is a genuinely good mechanic and the obvious intent - or delete the dial.

The factory's only adjustable dial does nothing

The factory presents four "Construction Points" properties. Three are read-outs derived from Size. The fourth is the only slider - and it is not connected.

The DataDict that becomes the IndustryAtb rate table lists exactly three keys - component-construction, installation-construction, ordnance-construction. Fighter points are not among them. And there is no fighter-construction industry type in the game at all: the five types are refining, component-construction, installation-construction, ordnance-construction and ship-assembly. So the dial has nowhere to go even if it were wired. It is a 0-1,000 slider, on the most-built installation in the game, that writes nothing and costs nothing.

Which makes it the cleanest decision in the door: fighters are either a production type or they are not. If they are, add the industry type and derive the points from Size like the other three - one line each, and the factory's cost structure already handles it. If they are not, delete the dial. What it must not stay is the one thing a player can adjust on a factory, doing nothing. Note the shipped default is 0, so nothing in the game is currently wrong because of it - this is a UI honesty problem, not a balance one.

What this kills

bunker - Mass = 50000, so a perfect fortress costs what a token one does

LocalFortify (0 -> 1) and AdjacentProjection (0 -> 1) are both free. The shipped bunker fortifies its own region by 0.25 and projects 0.12 to neighbours; a player-designed one can do 1.00 and 1.00 - total local fortification and full cover projected to every adjacent region - for the identical 50 tonnes, 50 crew and 200 credits. And this is precisely where the Defense door's ruling landed: fortification was cut from Defense because it is infrastructure, not a combat defence - correctly - and it arrived here unpriced. Plus TileFootprint (1 -> 40), which is the same cost-with-no-benefit dial the building foundation has: a bigger footprint is a bigger target on the ground map and buys nothing, so the minimum strictly dominates.

infrastructure - Mass = 1000 AND BuildPointCost = 100, with six free dials

Support Colonists 0-5,000 - Storage Amount 0-5,000 - Support Capacity 0-10,000 - Housing Comfort 0-50 - and the gravity and pressure tolerance ranges. Two constants in the cost block make all six free at once, and CreditCost is 0, so the most load-bearing installation in the colony game costs a flat tonne and a hundred build points whatever you ask of it. Most of those dials belong to the Civic door (housing, population support, tolerances) and are covered there; the one that is Industrial's is Support Capacity - the InfrastructureCapacityAtb that every other installation on the colony draws against. It is the colony's equivalent of a chassis budget, and it is free.

Cost Per Day and Fighter Construction Points - two dials that write nothing

dead dials - as opposed to free ones. Together with LogiBaseAtb.LogisticCapacity from the Logistical door, that is three dead dials across seven doors, and all three have the same tell: a real slider in the designer, a real field on the attribute, and no consumer. A grep for the field name returns only its own declaration and its own constructor.

And one thing that could have been a leak and is not. The shipyard has two dials, and the naive reading is that Crew Size is a free output multiplier - it drives both Component Construction Points (x0.01) and Ship Assembly Points (x0.02) while Mass reads only Slip Size. But CrewReq is PropertyValue('Crew Size') - the dial itself, one for one. So the output is paid for in the currency that is actually scarce, rather than in mass. Which is the seven-channels rule working correctly for once: the question is never "is it in the mass formula" but "which of the seven channels, and is it one the player is constrained by?" - and for a planet-side shipyard, people bind harder than kilograms.

The rule this door finally makes mechanical

Seven doors in, every leak found has had the same signature. Industrial is the door that makes it unmistakable, because here the honest and broken templates sit in the same file.

A dial is FREE exactly when the template's Mass formula does not read it.
Because all seven cost channels - Mass - Volume - CrewReq - ResearchCost - CreditCost - BuildPointCost - ResourceCost - are computed from [Mass] in almost every template, the mass formula is the single point of control.
Failure shapeWhat it looks likeWhere it has turned up
A. Mass = a constantevery dial on the template is free, all at onceresearch-lab - bunker - infrastructure - station-chassis - building-foundation - all four ground frames - troop-bay - space-port
B. Mass = a DIFFERENT dialyou declare your own price, independent of capabilityall six broken Enhancers (CarryMass / Cadre Mass / Automation Mass) - the ship hull's Mass Budget beside Hull Mass
C. the dial writes nothinga separate bug - it makes the cheapest setting dominant insteadFighter Construction Points - Cost Per Day - LogiBaseAtb - Size on a ground frame - the fire-control pair
So the gauge is a dozen lines, and it is the highest-leverage test in the project. Load the base mod, and for every template, for every property whose GuiHint starts with GuiSelection (i.e. a real player dial): assert that the property's name appears in at least one of the seven cost formulas, or in the argument list of an AtbConstrArgs (so it reaches the simulation), and flag it if it appears in neither. That single test catches shape A, shape B and shape C at once - it would have found every leak in all seven doors, and it fails on any new one somebody adds two years from now. It needs an explicit allow-list for deliberate exceptions, and writing that list is the design review.

And it is the answer to the honest objection about all of this work. Six doors of hand-audited findings do not stay found - the next person adds a template and the audit rots. A test does not rot. This one is cheap, it is data-only (no engine change, no save-format risk), and it turns "somebody should check the dials cost something" into a red CI run.

Prediction check, and it is the most useful score yet. The build plan said: "Healthy. Expect rate <-> efficiency as the shape, and the finding to be about missing costs rather than missing dials." - Healthy (seven of ten templates honest, the best door of the seven derived). Missing costs exactly right. Rate <-> efficiency , and instructively: industry has no internal trade and does not need one, because it is a pure scale dial and the correct test for a scale dial is whether benefit / cost stays flat. It does, on all nine honest rows. What the prediction missed entirely was the two dead dials - which is the same thing the Sensors prediction missed, and the same thing the Logistical one missed. Standing lesson: predictions catch the SHAPE of a door and reliably miss its dead code. Only a grep finds a dial with no consumer.

Where the door stands

One question - how much plant, and of what kind? One choice (mine - refine - manufacture - assemble ships - research - construct - fortify - lift) and one size slider, plus crew on a shipyard. It reproduces all ten templates.

The headline is genuinely good news, which has not happened yet in this campaign: seven of the ten templates are correct. Output is linear in the dial, mass is linear in the dial, and capability-per-tonne is flat - so there is no dominant setting anywhere in the mining, refining, manufacturing, construction or lift chain. The economy spine is sound. And the two mines independently satisfy the justification rule without anyone having designed for it.

Three templates fail, and they fail identically: research-lab, bunker and infrastructure each have a hard-coded number where the mass formula should read a dial, which makes every dial on them free simultaneously. The worst is the lab, because research is what gates every other door and a hundredfold research rate costs nothing.

Plus two dead dials: the factory's Fighter Construction Points - the only adjustable dial it presents besides Size, absent from the industry rate dictionary, with no fighter-construction industry type existing for it to feed - and the lab's Cost Per Day, stored on the attribute and read by nothing.

And the door's real deliverable is a rule that has become mechanical. Every leak in seven doors is one of two shapes: Mass = a constant or Mass = a different dial. Which means a test can find them: for every GuiSelection* property in every template, assert its name appears in at least one of the seven cost formulas or in an AtbConstrArgs - and flag it if it appears in neither. Data-only, no engine change, no save risk, a dozen lines. It would have found every finding in this campaign, and it does not rot the way a hand audit does. If only one thing gets built out of all eight doors, I would build that.

Two decisions are yours: whether fighters are a production type (add the industry type and derive the points from Size, or delete the dial), and whether a lab's Cost Per Day should actually bill the colony - it is a good mechanic and clearly the original intent, but it is currently a slider in front of an unread field.
